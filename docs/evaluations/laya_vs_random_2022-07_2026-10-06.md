# Laya vs. random selector, downstream pilot: July 2022 (2026-10-06)

**Plan:** `laya_vs_random_plan_2026-10-06.md`, frozen before either run.

**Scope:** one fresh 20-session episode per arm, over the same 48-name
pool, with the same Hermes setup.

**What this report is not:**
- not a significance test;
- not a causal claim;
- not evidence about other months.

> **Corrected 2026-10-07.** An evidence review (§11) verified identity
> leaks in the prompts and found that every BUY's stop and nearly every
> target sat on maverick's unlabeled synthetic ±5%/±10% levels. It also
> found several conclusions below overstated. Those conclusions are
> corrected in place (marked *corrected*). All measured numbers are
> unchanged, because reconciliation found no numerical error.

## Verdicts

These are four separate questions. *Corrected 2026-10-07.*

| Question | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Operational correctness** | **Yes, both arms, for what the experiment controlled.** 20/20 sessions each, no halt, no selector failure, no unavailable card. Hermes's candidates equalled each arm's selection on all 40 arm-days, in neutral alias order. No Laya score, rank or arm identity appeared in any prompt. Both arms used the same alias map, and all 1,007 historical fetches both arms made returned identical data. Preserved runs are unchanged. |
| **Selection evidence** | **Inconclusive.** On the unvalidated v0.2 review heuristic, Laya's shortlists hit the eligible base rate (35.5% vs 32.8%), as random's did (34.0% vs 33.1%). That is limited evidence, not proof that Laya is equivalent to random. Chance-sized overlap with one random draw is expected for *any* fixed top-10, so it is not a test of selection quality. Low scores (0.14–0.34) do not by themselves invalidate the ranking; the earlier calibration and framing concerns still apply. |
| **Trading outcome** | **The Laya arm had the better observed outcome:** +8.26% vs +4.02%, closed trades 7/8 vs 2/5 wins. **Its cause is unidentified.** The random arm was in cash for its first 7 sessions; the Laya arm was invested from 07-05 into a rally. Earlier investment could be a pathway through which selection matters, or it could be Hermes's path and timing variance. One path per arm cannot separate these, and cannot establish superiority, equivalence or no benefit. |
| **Integrity limitations** | **Material, and they differ by arm** (§11). Verified identity cues reached 8 of 20 Laya and 14 of 20 random decision prompts, plus 1 Laya gate prompt. All 45 BUY stops sat on an unlabeled synthetic `close × 0.95` level, although the rules require a "real technical level". Performance conclusions from this pair, or any run using the same packets, need these repaired first. |

- **Random:** 28 rows = 27 + 1.
  - BUYs: 10 + 13.

  cycle evidence (20 / 20) match the ledgers.

**Causes, rechecked against current code** (*corrected 2026-10-07*; the
earlier "raw prices plus 5 bps slippage" explanation was wrong for this
code):
- **Slippage is already reserved.** Current sizing
  (`required_entry_quantity` → `_expected_buy_fill_price`) reserves the
  5 bps fill markup at decision time, and Hermes's quantity must equal
  that exact whole-share size.
- **All 15 fill-time §2b rejections** were at or below the cap when sized:
  19.87–20.00% of decision-day equity.
  - **12 are next-day price drift:** the implied fill price was 0.5–2.0%
    above the reserved estimate.
  - **3 are unresolved:** the implied drift was ≤ 0.2%, and equity at the
    09:45 fill was not recorded, so an equity change can't be excluded.
- **The §6a rejection** (stop 2.32% below entry) is also next-morning
  drift. The stop had been set exactly 5% below the decision price.
- **The 4 decision-time blocks** are Hermes's own arithmetic: the quantity
  was off by one share from the required size.

Sizing exactly to the cap leaves no room for an overnight move. That
design property, not slippage, drives these rejections in both arms.

## 9. Limitations

**Design:**
- one path per arm and one month;
- stochastic Hermes decisions;
- one random seed;
- portfolios diverge after day 1, so prompts and eligible sets differ.

**Universe:**
- a retrospective fixed universe, chosen with present-day knowledge and
  partly qualified on retrospective availability. It is survivorship- and
  selection-biased.
- historical news was retrieved today.

**Integrity defects** (*corrected 2026-10-07*; these are not "shared,
equal" dampeners):
- **Identity cues reached prompts at different rates per arm** (§11.1).
- **Unlabeled synthetic levels** were offered as technical levels in both
  arms. Every stop used one (§11.2).
- **Cap rejections** come from at-the-cap sizing plus next-day drift
  (§3).

**No significance or causal claim is made.** One path per arm cannot
establish superiority, equivalence or no benefit, in either direction.

## 10. Recommendation (*corrected 2026-10-07*)

No further run now. Repair two integrity defects first (§11.6):
- the masking dictionary and its detection;
- labelling or removing the synthetic levels.

Only then would a further run answer a well-posed question.

Selection quality is **not** settled by this pair. The heuristic evidence
is weak and unvalidated, and the overlap measure cannot test it. Laya
stays in the project as an experimental component; an unproven increment
is not a reason to remove it.

### 11.1 Identity leakage

**Path:**
- **Masker:** `_names_for_masking` builds each name's variants from the
  legal name (suffix-stripped), `_BRAND_OVERRIDES` (3 baseline names) and
  the universe's `brands`. `RunMasking.mask_text` substitutes them.
- **Checker:** `RunMasking.assert_clean` searches for the **same
  dictionary** (`self._names`), so it **cannot detect any variant the
  dictionary lacks**. `anonymize.py` states this ceiling itself. The
  earlier audit's H4 found the same class in the V1/V2 baselines.
- **What the prompt shows:** for each candidate, the first 3 masked
  headlines, truncated to 110 characters, with no summaries. The pre-fill
  gate prompts quote headlines and article text.

**Missing variant categories found in text that reached Hermes:**
- abbreviation;
- partial-name residue (a leftover word after the masked portion);
- former legal name;
- brand or label;
- subsidiary;
- deal counterparty;
- facility.

| | Laya | Random |
|---|---|---|
| Decision prompts with a **verified** cue (the cue identifies the company whose block it appears in) | **8 of 20** (sessions 5, 12, 13, 14, 15, 18, 19, 25) | **14 of 20** (sessions 6, 8, 11–15, 18, 20, 22, 26–29) |
| Distinct companies identifiable that way | 6 | 8 |
| Pre-fill gate prompts with a verified cue | 1 of 7 (session 8; a counterparty purchase report that also pins the period) | 0 of 5 |
| Prompts with only **speculative** cues | 5 | 8 |
| Strings found only in source evidence, not in any prompt | 1 brand-like token, 1 session, never shown | the same token, 2 sessions |

**What counts as speculative:**
- a generic corporate-suffix residue;
- a sector peer or tenant named in the same block;
- a headline about a different product that shares a brand word.

**Tickers and full legal names:** none reached any prompt (scanned).

**Did leaks change decisions? Not established.**
- **Responses:** across 148 stored model-written texts, Hermes never
  wrote a real company name or repeated a cue string. That covers decision
  responses, gate replies, trade notes and insights.
- **Same-session trading on a cued name:** happened once in the Laya arm
  (a SELL) and twice in the random arm (a SELL and a BUY).
- **Conclusion:** the evidence shows **exposure**, not recognition or
  influence, and it cannot rule out silent recognition.
- **The arms were not equally affected:** exposure differs because their
  selected names differ.

**The pre-launch stop-condition amendment** (`f2e6819`) narrowed "a leak
into a prompt" to tickers, full names and Laya/arm data. The blind review
had just shown abbreviated and partial names surviving in headlines.
- **It was made before launch and recorded openly**, and the frozen plan
  is left as it was.
- **It did weaken the evidence standard.** Under the original wording,
  these verified leaks would have stopped or delayed the experiment for
  repair.
- **Its justification ("symmetric across arms") was wrong.**

### 11.2 Support/resistance provenance

**Source:** `maverick/technical/analysis.py::support_resistance`.
- **Support** = {30-bar low, `round(close × 0.95, 2)`,
  `round(close × 0.90, 2)`}.
- **Resistance** = {30-bar high, `round(close × 1.05, 2)`,
  `round(close × 1.10, 2)`}.
- `sr_lookback` is 30.

**Path to the prompt:**
1. `run_backtest.fetch_technicals` passes all three of each list.
2. `build_prompt` prints `support [...]  resistance [...]` with **no
   label distinguishing synthetic values from observed extremes**.

**RULES and AGENTS conflict:** RULES.md §6a requires the stop at a "real
technical level … not an arbitrary percentage", and AGENTS.md says
"never fabricate … a level".

**Measured on the stored candidate packets (both arms):**

| | Laya | Random |
|---|---|---|
| Support values: synthetic / observed | 401 / 199 | 402 / 198 |
| Resistance values: synthetic / observed / ambiguous | 398 / 199 / 2 | 399 / 198 / 2 |
| Nearest support below price: synthetic / observed | 178 / 22 | 156 / 44 |
| BUY stops exactly on a supplied level (within 1¢) | **22 of 22, all on the synthetic 0.95 level** | **23 of 23, all synthetic** |
| BUY targets exactly on a supplied level | 21 synthetic (1.10×) + 1 observed | 21 synthetic + 2 observed |
| Hermes's `technical_basis` / stated reward:risk | "multiple" on all 22 / mostly 2.00 | "multiple" on all 23 / mostly 2.00 |

**Classification:** a value is synthetic if it is within 1¢ of
`round(price × k, 2)`. It is ambiguous where the observed extreme
coincides with a synthetic value.

**Consequence:** in practice, the trades are a fixed 5% stop and 10%
target, recorded as technical levels. This is a **numeric match, not
proof of motive**: Hermes may have chosen these values for other reasons.
The fact that matters is that the packet gave no way to tell a real level
from a synthetic one, so the claim of a technical stop is not supported
by the inputs.

**Scope:** this source path is shared by every backtest that uses these
packets, including the V1/V2 baselines. That is likely but **not verified
here**.

### 11.6 Recommended repair block

The smallest repair. No strategy or rule change, and no new run until it
passes.

**1. Masking**
- **Fix:** add the missing variant categories for the active pools to the
  masking configuration (brands, former names, affiliates, subsidiaries,
  known counterparties).
- **Fix:** add an **independent** detector built from a separate
  cue list, not the masker's own dictionary. It flags (or fails) any prompt
  containing a known cue.
- **Regression tests:**
  - each verified cue category from this review stays masked or is
    detected;
  - the detector catches a variant that the masker's dictionary lacks;
  - ordinary prose is not corrupted.

**2. Levels**
- **Fix:** label each support and resistance value in the packet by
  provenance (observed 30-bar extreme vs synthetic percentage), or stop
  sending the synthetic values.
- **Fix:** journal which level a stop or target used.
- **Regression tests:**
  - a prompt-rendering test that the labels or omission are present;
  - a test that a stop equal to an unlabeled synthetic value cannot be
    recorded as a technical level.

**3. Verification:** re-run the read-only leak and provenance scans from
this review against stored evidence. No Hermes call is needed.

**Further runs:** none is warranted now. After the repair, the exact
question a run could answer is: *with identity cues removed and
level provenance explicit, do Laya-selected and random shortlists lead to
different entry timing and outcomes over repeated paths on identical
shortlists?* That needs a separately authorized design.

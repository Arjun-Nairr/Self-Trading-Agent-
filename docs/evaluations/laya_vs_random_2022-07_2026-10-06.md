# Laya vs. random selector, downstream pilot: July 2022 (2026-10-06)

**Plan:** `laya_vs_random_plan_2026-10-06.md`, frozen before either run.

**Scope:** one fresh 20-session episode per arm, over the same 48-name
pool, with the same Hermes setup.

**What this report is not:**
- not a significance test;
- not a causal claim;
- not evidence about other months.

## Verdicts

These are three separate questions.

| Question | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Operational correctness** | **Yes, both arms.** 20/20 sessions each, no halt, no selector failure, no unavailable card. Hermes's candidates equalled each arm's selection on all 40 arm-days, in neutral alias order. No Laya score, rank or arm identity appeared in any prompt. Both arms used the same alias map, and all 1,007 historical fetches both arms made returned identical data. Preserved runs are unchanged. |
| **Selection plausibility** | **Laya not distinguishable from random.** Laya's shortlists hit the review heuristic at the eligible base rate (35.5% vs 32.8%), as random's did (34.0% vs 33.1%). Daily overlap with the random draw sits at chance (mean 1.85 of 10; about 2 expected). On the three blind-reviewed sessions, both arms caught the same small share of the names I had judged review-worthy. Laya's scores again sat at 0.14–0.34, never above 0.5, with rank-10/11 gaps of at most 0.006. |
| **Trading outcomes** | **The Laya arm did better on this one path:** +8.26% vs +4.02%, closed trades 7/8 vs 2/5 wins. **This is not attributable to Laya.** The random arm was in cash for its first 7 sessions of a rallying month: it made 1 BUY proposal in its first 5 sessions, and its early queued orders were vetoed or capped. With one stochastic Hermes path per arm and no selection difference shown above, the gap is best read as path and timing variance. |

### Post-hoc diagnostics (labelled as such)

These were inspected after the runs. None of them is a verdict on a
company: no untraded name is called a bad pick, and no counterfactual
trade is described.
- **Random's idle start.**
  - **Proposals:** 0, 0, 1, 1, 0 and 1 BUY in the first six sessions.
  - **What happened to them:** the 07-06 proposal was vetoed by overnight
    news before its fill. The 07-07 and 07-11 ones were rejected at §2b
    (20.13% and 20.05%) at the next open.
  - **First fills:** 07-13.
  - **Effect:** the Laya arm was invested from 07-05 into a rally, which
    is most of the return and exposure gap.
- **Laya's largest day:** +$3,040 on 07-28, from open positions in the
  rally. Seven of its 8 closed trades were winners; random's were 2 of 5.
- **Data quality, both arms:**
  - **Weak levels:** maverick support and resistance often sit about
    −5.3% / +5.0% from price for most names (placeholder-like), so the
    packets carry weak level information.
  - **Masking ceiling:** documented and recorded before launch.
    Abbreviated or partial company-name strings reached 3 of 20 Laya
    prompts and 7 of 20 random prompts. The strings are of the same kind
    in both arms, and the counts follow which names each arm selected.
    No ticker or full name reached a prompt (checked by scan).

## 9. Limitations

**Design:**
- one path per arm and one month;
- stochastic Hermes decisions;
- one random seed;
- portfolios diverge after day 1, so prompts and eligible sets differ.

**Universe:**
- a retrospective fixed universe, chosen with present-day knowledge and
  partly qualified on retrospective availability. It is survivorship- and
  selection-biased.
- historical news was retrieved today.

**Shared defects that dampen both arms:**
- weak support/resistance data;
- the masking ceiling;
- the sizing and slippage cap rejections.

**No significance or causal claim is made.** In particular, nothing here
shows that Laya's selection caused the higher return.

## 10. Recommendation

No automatic second month.

The open question this pair leaves is how much of the outcome gap is
Hermes path and timing variance. Another month of one path per arm cannot
answer it. It would need repeated paths over identical shortlists, which
would be a separately authorized design.

Selection quality is already answered for this setup: on every
selection-level measure, Laya matches random.
